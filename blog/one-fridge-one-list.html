One fridge, one list, no group chat

By Greg, founder of ok2eat

Last Saturday my wife sent me a text from Trader Joe's: "Did you already grab eggs?"

I had. Earlier that morning at Whole Foods. Two cartons of eggs were on their way home with me.

If you live with someone who shops, you know this pattern. You text. They text back twenty minutes later when their hands aren't full of bread. You guess. Sometimes you guess right. Sometimes you get home and discover you now have four heads of garlic.

ok2eat just shipped a fix for this in v1.1.0.

What's new

Multiple named shopping lists per household. Create a list for Costco, a list for Trader Joe's, a list for the weekday grab — whatever helps you keep things separate. Each list lives inside your household, so anyone you've invited can see and add to it.

Real-time sync across phones. When my wife adds avocados to the "Costco trip" list, they show up on my phone within a few seconds. No texts. No group chats. No manual updates. The list is the source of truth.

Creator initials next to each item. Every item on a shared list shows a small green badge with the initial of whoever added it. So you can tell at a glance whether the bag of frozen blueberries was you or her — useful when you're staring at the freezer aisle wondering if anyone's actually going to use them.

Order in one tap. When the list looks right, hit "Order N items" and ok2eat opens Instacart, Amazon, or Walmart with everything pre-loaded into the search. Done in 30 seconds.

Why this matters

Two things stop happening once you wire this up.

Duplicate purchases. No more "did we already get rice?" because the list is the answer. If it's checked off, it's coming. If it's not, it isn't.

Coordination overhead. Decisions move from "let me text and wait" to "I can see what's already happening." For two adults trading off shopping trips, that's the difference between project-managing your groceries and just buying them.

The real win is transparency. Before, if my wife added "ginger" to a list, I had to choose between buying it (in case she'd planned a recipe around it) or not buying it (in case it was already in the fridge). Now I can see who added it, when, and whether it's checked off — three pieces of context that make the decision obvious.

Try it

Open the Plan tab in ok2eat. Tap "+ New list" in the top right. Name it after a store, a trip, or a purpose. Tap Share at the top of any screen and invite your household. Start adding items.

If you're already using ok2eat for inventory tracking, this is just an upgrade on the same backend. If you're not — free on iPhone, and now also on the web.

Coming next

We're working on price comparison. When you're about to reorder a low-stock item, you'll see prices side-by-side across Instacart, Amazon, Walmart, and the local grocery stores within five miles of your home. Less waste, more savings — and smarter spending on what you actually buy.

— Greg
