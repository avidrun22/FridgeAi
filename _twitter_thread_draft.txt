Twitter/X thread draft — "what do we have to eat?" framing

================================================================
THREAD — 9 tweets
================================================================

1/9 (HOOK)
Every household asks the same question every night around 7pm:

"What do we have to eat?"

For years my wife and I couldn't answer it. So we ordered takeout 4 nights a week instead.

Here's what changed. 🧵

————————————————————————————————

2/9
The pattern was always the same.

Open the fridge. Stare. Half-formed thoughts —
"is there chicken in there?"
"are those carrots still good?"
"what was I going to do with the lemons?"

After a minute, one of us caves: "want to just order something?"

————————————————————————————————

3/9
Meanwhile the fridge was full.

We'd bought groceries with real intentions three days earlier. The chicken was fine. The carrots were fine.

We just couldn't see what we had clearly enough to assemble a meal in the time we had patience for.

————————————————————————————————

4/9
Realized "what do we want for dinner?" is the wrong question.

It asks you to start from desire and work backward to what's possible.

Better question: "what do we HAVE to eat?"

Start with what's in the fridge. Prioritize what's about to spoil. Work forward.

————————————————————————————————

5/9
So I built ok2eat.

Snap a photo of the grocery receipt. The app reads every item, sets expiry estimates, populates inventory. ~10 seconds, no typing.

Open it at 6pm. Fridge is sorted by what's expiring soonest.

Right next to the list: 3 recipes built from those exact ingredients.

————————————————————————————————

6/9
The receipt scan is the unlock.

Every previous "track your fridge" app I tried failed because the data entry was annoying. A whiteboard, a notes app, Trello — they all collapsed within two weeks.

Photo of the receipt = entire grocery trip in your inventory. No manual entry, ever.

————————————————————————————————

7/9
Real-time shared fridge between phones.

My wife and I see the same inventory. If she used the rest of the lemons at lunch, my dinner plans automatically change.

No more "did we already buy eggs?" texts.

————————————————————————————————

8/9
Six months in, the numbers:

• Takeout: 4 nights/week → 1
• Grocery bill: down ~25%
• Trash bag on Sunday: mostly bones and packaging now, not produce

We don't decide what's for dinner anymore. The fridge does.

————————————————————————————————

9/9

If you and your partner have the same 7pm staring contest at the fridge, give it a try:
