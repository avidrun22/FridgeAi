### `/use-cases/stop-wasting-produce` — "Stop wasting produce"

**Headline:** Spinach goes bad in 5 days. Most people find out on day 7.

**Hook:**
Produce is the single most-wasted category in American kitchens. The USDA's research shows households throw out about 30% of every bag of greens, every bunch of cilantro, every clamshell of berries. Not because we meant to — because we forgot it was in there.

ok2eat ranks your fridge by what's about to spoil. The thing closest to going bad sits at the top of the Eat Me First tab. Tap it, see three recipes that use it — plus the four next-most-urgent things alongside it — so dinner uses what's about to go bad instead of what you'd have to buy.

**Features that earn this page:**
- **Eat Me First tab** — fridge ranked by urgency, top item is what to cook tonight
- **USDA-backed expiry dates** — calibrated shelf life for 1,900+ foods (cilantro: 1 week, bagged spinach: 5-7 days, hard cheese: 6 months)
- **Receipt scanning** — every produce item from your grocery run gets the correct expiry date automatically

**Real review (Doodlebug04 · App Store · May 2026):**
> "Great way to reduce waste and make yourself accountable for lost $$ while understanding your weekly grocery needs."

**CTA:** See what's about to go bad in your fridge — try the demo.

**Cross-links:** for-misfits-imperfect · cut-grocery-bill

### `/use-cases/cut-grocery-bill` — "How to cut your grocery bill"

**Headline:** The average US household throws away $1,866 of food a year. Yours doesn't have to.

**Hook:**
Groceries jumped 0.7% in April — the biggest one-month jump in nearly four years. But here's the part nobody talks about: 30% of what you buy gets thrown out before you eat it. So when the price of milk goes up 10%, your *effective* cost goes up much more than that, because you're paying inflated prices for groceries you don't even use.

ok2eat doesn't lower the price of milk. It makes sure you actually eat the milk before it goes bad. That's the real lever.

**Features that earn this page:**

- **Daily Digest** — morning email showing what's expiring this week so dinner planning starts there, not at the grocery store

### `/use-cases/for-batch-cooking` — "ok2eat for meal prep + batch cooking"

**Headline:** You cooked five pounds of chili on Sunday. Now what?

**Hook:**
Batch cooking is half the battle. The other half is remembering you have five containers of chili in the fridge before they hit day 6. The default expiry date in most apps is whatever the *ingredient* date was — so cooked chili inherits the raw beef's expiry, which is wrong. Cooked chili lasts 3-4 days; raw beef lasts 1-2.

ok2eat just shipped a Cooked status that gives leftovers their own timer. Tag your Sunday chili as Cooked → 4-day window starts that day → the leftover lands on your Eat Me First tab on Wednesday so you remember to eat it before Friday.

**Features that earn this page:**
- **Cooked status** — leftovers get a 3-5 day timer, not the raw ingredient's expiry. New in v1.22.
- **Partial Use Tracking** — used half the chili? Log it. Inventory stays accurate.
- **Eat Me First** — leftover chili pops to the top on day 3 so it doesn't become day-6 garbage

**CTA:** Tag your next batch as Cooked — see how it changes the week.

**Cross-links:** for-families · stop-wasting-produce

**Headline:** Rescued produce shouldn't end up rescued again — by your trash.

Misfits Market and Imperfect Foods deliver "ugly" produce that would otherwise go to waste at the farm or distributor. Beautiful mission, fragile produce. The carrots are bendy when they arrive. The peppers have a soft spot. They need to be used in days, not weeks.

ok2eat assigns the right shorter shelf life to rescued produce so it doesn't sit in the bin until it's too late. The whole point of buying rescued food is *not* rescuing it from another trip to the trash. ok2eat closes that loop.
