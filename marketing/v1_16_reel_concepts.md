# v1.16 Reel concepts — high-impact, low-production

You're a solo founder, not a video producer. Everything here is shootable on your iPhone in ≤15 minutes with no extra gear. Three concepts ranked by ease-vs-impact; pick one to shoot Day 0 or +1.

## The universal rules (apply to all three)

These are the highest-leverage moves regardless of which concept you pick. Skipping them is the difference between a reel that gets 200 views and one that gets 2,000+.

1. **Hook in the first 1.5 seconds.** IG/TikTok algorithms test retention in the first second. If the opening frame doesn't visually answer "why should I keep watching?", you lose 70% of viewers before you say anything. The hook can be a question on the screen, a startling number, or a specific action (opening a fridge counts).
2. **Vertical 9:16, never landscape.** Native iPhone vertical (1080×1920 or 1920×1080 doesn't matter — IG handles cropping if vertical). Landscape reels get downranked.
3. **Caption every spoken word + key UI states.** 85% of IG/TikTok viewers watch with sound off. Use IG's auto-captions (Reel editor → Caption sticker) — it's free, accurate enough, and on-brand.
4. **Native phone aesthetic, not "production."** Hand-held, real fridge, real food, real app on the real phone. Lit by kitchen overhead is fine. Polished b-roll reads as ad and people scroll past. Authentic reads as advice.
5. **Sound on, but the music is structural, not decorative.** Use a trending-but-quiet audio from IG's library (look for ~10–20 sec clips that have a single beat-drop near the end). The beat-drop should land on your strongest visual moment (the app showing the answer). Don't talk over it — let captions carry the words.
6. **One concrete number anywhere in the reel.** "$1,866/yr." "660 foods." "3 days left." Specifics are sticky. Generalities ("a lot of food") are forgettable.
7. **End on a CTA, not a fade-out.** Last 2 seconds: ok2eat.com URL or App Store badge with text "Free, link in bio." Many viewers tap before fully understanding what they tapped — make sure the destination is unambiguous.
8. **Post at 6pm PT.** That's when people are actually standing in front of their fridge wondering what to cook. Both algorithm engagement and contextual relevance are highest at dinner time.

## Concept 1 — "POV: standing at the fridge" (RECOMMENDED, easiest)

**Total runtime:** 12–15 seconds
**Effort:** 10 minutes, one take, no editing beyond captions

### Shot list

| Sec | What you film | Caption overlay |
|-----|---------------|-----------------|
| 0–2 | Hand opens fridge (POV, your real fridge, no staging). Hold for a beat. | *"What do we cook tonight?"* |
| 3–4 | Close fridge, look slightly defeated. Phone is in your other hand. | *(no caption — let the silence land)* |
| 5–7 | Phone screen fills frame. Tap ok2eat. Eat Me First tab is already open. | *"ok2eat just tells you."* |
| 8–11 | Finger taps the top item (a real expiring thing — milk, spinach, chicken). Recipe sheet slides up. Scroll once to show the recipe list. | *"3 recipes per item. Uses what's about to spoil."* |
| 12–14 | Cut to the v1.16 IG launch image you already have at `_v1_16_ig_image.png`. | *"Free → ok2eat.com"* |

### Why this works

- Hook is a real moment everyone recognizes (the fridge-staring). High retention.
- The phone-tap-to-recipe sequence is the actual product value moment — viewers see the unlock happen.
- No script. No B-roll. No editing beyond trim + captions.
- The "ok2eat just tells you" caption is the entire reposition compressed into four words.

### Tips for shooting

- **Lock exposure before opening the fridge.** Tap-and-hold on your kitchen counter, then tap "AE/AF lock." This stops the iPhone from blowing out the highlights when the fridge light hits the lens.
- **Wipe the phone screen** before the second half so it doesn't show fingerprints on the dark UI.
- **Use a tripod or counter prop** for the phone-screen segment. Hand-shake on UI close-ups looks unprofessional.
- **Shoot in iPhone's Cinematic mode** if your phone supports it — the background blur on the fridge interior shot reads as polished without effort.

## Concept 2 — "The number reveal" (medium effort, good if you have 20 min)

**Total runtime:** 20–25 seconds
**Effort:** 20 minutes, three short cuts, light editing

### Shot list

| Sec | What you film | Caption overlay |
|-----|---------------|-----------------|
| 0–2 | Slow zoom on a pile of food on the counter (anything you'd reasonably toss — wilting greens, an old block of cheese, a half-loaf of bread). | *"The average US household throws this out every year:"* |
| 3–5 | Big text on screen: **$1,866** | (the number IS the caption — make it huge, centered, fade in) |
| 6–9 | Cut to your fridge actually populated, then the ok2eat Eat Me First screen with the top item highlighted. | *"ok2eat tells you what to eat first — before it goes bad."* |
| 10–14 | Show one recipe card. Show the "Use this milk" button. | *"3 recipes per item. Uses what's expiring."* |
| 15–20 | Cut to the Impact Dashboard showing dollars saved. | *"Live count of what you've rescued."* |
| 21–24 | Logo + URL. | *"Free → ok2eat.com"* |

### Why this works

- Number-based hook is high-retention on TikTok/IG (people stop scrolling for surprising stats).
- $1,866 is sourced (USDA / ReFED 2024) — works as both a hook AND credibility signal.
- The dashboard reveal at the end gives the viewer something concrete to imagine themselves achieving.

### Tips

- **Source the number on-screen** for trust: small text below "$1,866" reading "USDA / ReFED 2024" in DM Mono.
- **The 'food on counter' shot is the riskiest** — if it looks too staged, you lose. Aim for actually-spoiling-ish (or simulate by lightly misting cheese for a tired look).

## Concept 3 — "Tour of v1.16 tabs in 15 seconds" (most product-focused)

**Total runtime:** 15 seconds
**Effort:** 15 minutes, screen-recording only (no IRL footage)

### Shot list

| Sec | What you film | Caption overlay |
|-----|---------------|-----------------|
| 0–1 | iPhone screen-record. Open ok2eat. Fridge tab is the landing. | *"Your fridge is just a list, right? Wrong."* |
| 2–5 | Tap Eat First tab. Show the ranked list. Scroll once. | *"NEW: Eat Me First — sorted by urgency."* |
| 6–8 | Tap an item. Recipe sheet slides up. Show 3 recipes. | *"Tap any item, get 3 recipes."* |
| 9–11 | Tap Dashboard tab. Show $84 saved. | *"NEW: Dashboard — live count of what you've rescued."* |
| 12–14 | Tap Settings → show dietary chips lit up. | *"Set your diet once. Every recipe respects it."* |
| 15 | End frame: ok2eat.com | *"Free, iPhone + web."* |

### Why this works

- Pure product-tour reels work well for utility apps because viewers see exactly what they're getting.
- Each tab gets ~3 seconds — enough to register but not enough to drag.
- "NEW" badges on the captions match the App Store What's New copy, so cross-platform consistency.

### Tips

- **Record at 60fps** if your phone supports it — smoother UI transitions read as "high quality."
- **Use the iOS native screen recorder** (Control Center → record button). Don't use a 3rd-party recorder; they add watermarks or compress badly.
- **Disable notifications** on your phone before recording. A surprise text banner mid-reel kills the take.

## Comparison

| Concept | Effort | Production polish | Hook strength | Best for |
|---------|--------|------------------|---------------|----------|
| 1. POV fridge | 10 min | Low (intentional) | High — real moment | First-time viewers, IG/TikTok cold reach |
| 2. Number reveal | 20 min | Medium | Very high — stat-based | LinkedIn-style audience, share-worthy |
| 3. Tab tour | 15 min | High | Medium — product-focused | Returning users, app-curious viewers |

## My recommendation

**Shoot Concept 1.** It's the lowest-effort, highest-retention option for an indie launch. The "POV: standing at the fridge" moment is universally recognizable — every viewer has done that exact thing. It also reads as authentic (you, real fridge, real phone) which is what the brand voice in `brand_context.md` already trades on.

If Concept 1 lands well in Day +1 numbers, follow up with Concept 3 on Day +5 (recycle the cross-post slot in the schedule). Concept 2 is best held for a non-launch week — it's stronger as a standalone "stat-based" reel that doesn't need v1.16 context to work.

## After you shoot — checklist before posting

- [ ] Captions on every spoken word + every UI state change
- [ ] 9:16 vertical, full screen on iPhone
- [ ] First 1.5 sec has a visual hook (no logo lead-in)
- [ ] One concrete number visible somewhere
- [ ] End frame has URL or App Store badge for ≥2 sec
- [ ] Trending-but-quiet IG audio attached
- [ ] Caption length under 100 chars (gets full-display in feed)
- [ ] Posted at 6pm PT, not 9am

## Cross-posting to Pinterest

Pinterest accepts vertical video as "Video Pins" — same 9:16 ratio as your reel. After posting to IG/TikTok, drop the same MP4 into Pinterest with:

- **Title (100 char):** "What to eat first — before it goes bad (ok2eat 1.16)"
- **Description:** the IG caption (below)
- **Destination URL:** `https://ok2eat.com/blog/what-to-eat-first.html`
- **Board:** "ok2eat — App + updates"

Pinterest video pins keep driving traffic for weeks, not days — worth the 30 seconds it takes to cross-post.

## Caption to use under the reel post

Reuse this exact caption for IG, TikTok, Reels, and Pinterest Video Pins. Keep it short — viewers don't read long captions on short-form video.

```
You open the fridge. You don't know what to cook. ok2eat 1.16 just tells you what to eat first — before it goes bad. Free → ok2eat.com
```

Three sentences, 144 chars, no hashtags (those go in the first comment for IG, or skip on TikTok). 1 emoji-free, matches the voice in the rest of the launch.
