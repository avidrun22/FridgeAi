## Video 2 — "One list, two phones"

**Length:** ~25 seconds.
**Aspect ratio:** 9:16 vertical, with the two-phone split happening as a side-by-side composition inside the vertical frame (not a horizontal split — vertical works better on social).
**Goal:** Show the v1.1.0+ household collaboration in real time. This is the most visually striking thing the app does — two phones updating each other live is rare in a category dominated by single-user note apps.
**Use:** IG Reels, TikTok, X. Heavier social play; less ideal for website hero (too much going on for a passive viewer).

### Storyboard

| Frame | Time | What's on screen | Text overlay |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.0–2.0s | Two phones, side by side in vertical frame. Both showing ok2eat Plan tab → empty "Trader Joe's run" list. Caption labels above each phone: "MY PHONE" and "MY PARTNER'S PHONE" (or pick names — Greg / Partner). | "Did you already buy eggs?" |

| 4 | 6.0–10.0s | Right phone (Partner): finger taps "Add multiple items" → modal opens, types "milk\nbread\navocados". Save. All three appear on both phones. | (no overlay — let it speak) |
| 5 | 10.0–13.0s | Left phone: finger taps the recently-added chip "eggs" — second eggs appears, then user laughs and removes it. | "Add many at once." |
| 6 | 13.0–16.0s | Both phones: someone checks off "milk" on the left phone. It animates to bottom + collapses on BOTH phones into "Got 1 item ▾". | "Bought items get out of the way." |
| 7 | 16.0–20.0s | Left phone: tap green "Order N items" button → retailer picker → tap Instacart. Right phone shows the list still. | "And one tap to order." |

### Production notes
- The split-screen composition is the make-or-break visual. Use a vertical frame (9:16) and stack the two phones side by side inside it with horizontal padding. Each phone is roughly 40% of frame width, with labels above each.
- Time the actions on the two phones precisely — the right phone's "eggs appear" should land within 1 second of the left phone's add. This sells the real-time-sync story. If you can't hit it live, edit it tight in post.
- No dialogue. Music should pulse with the actions — something with a steady beat, not melodic, so the syncs feel deliberate.
- This is the only video that benefits from a touch of motion design (small green flash when items appear on the second phone). CapCut has "transition" effects that approximate this.

### Production notes
- The hero frame is the receipt-to-data transformation in frame 3. Frame the shot so the receipt and the phone screen are both visible — viewer's eye tracks from the printed receipt to the parsed result on screen.

- Tight, fast pacing on this one. 15 seconds total, no breathing room. Fast cuts.
- Music: more energetic / drum-forward than the other two. This is your "you won't believe this" cold-traffic clip.

## Tools + workflow recap

**Recording:**
- **iOS Simulator** for the cleanest pixel-perfect output: `xcrun simctl io booted recordVideo --codec h264 ~/Desktop/clip.mov`. Ctrl-C to stop.
- **Real iPhone** for the splash/intro frames where you want a "real device" feel — use iOS built-in screen recording (Settings → Control Center → add Screen Recording).
- **Two simulators for video #2:** Boot a second device with `xcrun simctl boot "iPhone 15"` (or whatever device IDs you want), sign each into a different ok2eat account in the same household. Use macOS's "Tile Window" to position them side by side, then screen-record the whole desktop region with QuickTime.

**Editing:**
- **iMovie** for the website hero (16:9 horizontal cut of video #1). Already on your Mac.
- **CapCut** for vertical/social cuts. Free, much better caption auto-generation, more transition effects. ~15 min learning curve.

**Captions baked in:** Critical. ~85% of Reels/TikTok/Shorts viewers watch muted. CapCut auto-generates and color-codes captions; review for accuracy and any auto-correct mistakes.

**Aspect ratio production order:** Record once vertical (9:16). Export the master cut. Then crop a 16:9 version from the same source for website hero. Don't try to record horizontal first — you can't crop UP from horizontal to vertical without losing key UI areas.

**Distribution checklist after you have the three masters:**
- [ ] Upload all three to YouTube as Shorts (build subscribers — important for Impact reapply gate)
- [ ] Cross-post each to IG Reels and TikTok (use Buffer or post manually)
- [ ] X: native upload (don't link to YouTube — algorithm penalty)
- [ ] Embed video #1 in `ok2eat.html` hero (16:9 horizontal cut, autoplay muted loop)
- [ ] Embed all three on the blog `/#how` section as a stitched 60s tour
- [ ] Update App Store Connect listing with the receipt-scan clip if Apple accepts video previews (they do — see App Preview specs)

**Time budget:** ~4 hours start to finish. Recording 60-90 min (with retakes), editing ~30 min/video × 3 = 90 min, cross-posting 30 min.
